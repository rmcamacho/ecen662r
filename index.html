About the course

Quantum computers are now available through the cloud, and dozens of companies are building them. This course covers the engineering and the algorithms, and every other Wednesday it turns to a Harvard business case about a real quantum company.

  • Run circuits on IBM quantum processors through a class account.
  • Qubits, entanglement, Shor, Grover, and variational algorithms, with the linear algebra behind them.
  • Superconducting, trapped-ion, neutral-atom, photonic, and spin qubits: how each works, how each fails, and what error correction costs.
  • Why the largest quantum computers will be networks of smaller ones, linked by light.
  • Harvard Business School cases on D-Wave, Google Quantum AI, and Horizon Quantum, taught by Jefferson Moss.
  • A semester project with business students: due diligence on a quantum company, presented to a mock investment committee.

Who can take it

Students

Graduate students and advanced undergraduates in electrical and computer engineering, physics, computer science, and math. Business and MBA students may enroll with instructor consent.

Prerequisites

Linear algebra (MATH 213 or 215) and some programming. No prior quantum mechanics.

Cost

Technical materials are free online. One Harvard case packet, about $25.

Final

No final exam. Your team's final presentation and report take its place.
